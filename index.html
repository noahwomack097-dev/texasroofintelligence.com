01

Your roof does not keep water out.
Only the felt beneath it keeps water out.

Most homeowners assume the shingles on top of their roof are what keep water out. They are not. Beneath every roof is a layer of waterproof felt — and that felt is the only thing standing between your home and water damage. The shingles exist for one reason only: to protect the felt underneath.

02

Without strong protection above.
The felt will rip and tear apart and let water inside.

Waterproof felt is highly effective when it's intact. But it is not built to survive on its own. Hail punches through it. UV sunlight degrades it. Wind tears it. Without thick, healthy shingles absorbing those forces above it, the felt breaks down — and once it does, water moves straight into your home.

03

Your roof looks perfectly fine.
But it stopped protecting your home from water damage years ago.

Shingles rarely fall off or visibly break. They simply wear down over time and grow incredibly thin — losing the protective elements that shield the waterproof felt from hail, UV sunlight, and wind. From the outside, everything looks normal. Most homeowners only find out when it's too late and water damage has already happened.

Common concerns

Is the report really free?

Yes — completely. No hidden fees, no surprise charges. You get the full written report at no cost, regardless of whether you choose to work with us or not. It's yours to keep.

Will you pressure me to buy a roof?

If the potential water damage to your home doesn't make you want a roof, then I definitely can't — and I won't waste your time trying to convince you otherwise. It's your house and your investment. I can give you the facts to make the decision, but I can't make it for you.

How do I know I'm not being ripped off or tricked?

Everything goes in writing and we document the entire process with photos for you to look at. We will also show you the removed shingles and especially the removed felt — as that is the most important part. You're welcome to pull up a lawn chair and watch, though I wouldn't recommend it. The AC is a lot more comfortable.

How can I afford a roof if it needs to be replaced?

There are several ways to pay for a roof — cash, financing, and insurance. The most common in North Texas is through insurance, where you pay your deductible and the difference is handled for you.
